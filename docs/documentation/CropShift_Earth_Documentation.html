Summary

Problem. Climate data is everywhere, but people cannot tell a real change from a few unusual years, and a trend alone does not tell a farmer what to plant. The Middle East and North Africa is the most water-scarce region in the world, and about 1 in 6 Egyptian workers farms.

Solution. CropShift Earth is a 3D globe built on 25 years of NASA POWER data (2001 to 2025). Pick a measurement and any place or country to see how fast it changes, where, since when, and how strong the evidence is (“strong”, “moderate” or “not proven”). For 20 countries it turns those trends into farming answers: which of 40 crops fit each place today, in 2030 and in 2035 if current trends continue, irrigated or rain-fed.

Impact. Farmers, investors and planners see where farming potential is rising or falling, with its evidence, in time to adapt. Every number is checked against NASA’s own weather stations and satellite rain record.

Challenge fit. It answers each question of the challenge: what is changing, where, how much, whether it is significant, and the twist of opposite trends in one country (Sudan: cooling south, warming north).

2. What it does

Working features

  • Working Trend explorer, anywhere on Earth. Click a place or search a country or one of about 1,250 cities. For a point: chart, trend line, rate per decade, 95% range, evidence level, the year the change started, and for air temperature the same trend from NASA’s weather stations next to it. For a country or region: a map of every grid cell; proven cells are colored, the others are see-through.
  • Working Whole Earth layer. Air temperature for every land and sea cell at once: 2001 to 2025, about 29,600 cells proven rising and 900 proven falling of about 208,000.
  • Working Custom years. Any range of at least 10 years between 2001 and 2025.
  • Working Crops tab (20 countries of the Middle East and North Africa): which of 40 crops fit a place today, in 2030 and in 2035, irrigated or rain-fed, or where one crop fits across a country.
  • Working Farmland tab: where farming potential is rising or falling, with the label “if current trends continue” and a 95% range.
  • Working Decisions tab: ranked planning signals for investors and governments, each with its evidence.
  • Working How we know: a Methods page with every check, and a 60 second guided tour.

3. How it works

Data

NASA POWER monthly grids (built on NASA’s MERRA-2 reanalysis) at 0.5 x 0.625 degrees, 2001 to 2025, for six measurements. Any region is fetched live from NASA the first time and kept on disk; Egypt and the world air temperature grid ship with the app, so the demo works even if NASA is slow.

Is the change real? (trend and confidence)

For each place we take yearly means and measure the rate with the Theil-Sen slope. Confidence comes from the Mann-Kendall test with its variance corrected for year to year memory (effective sample size). On every map, the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction accounts for testing hundreds of places at once. The Pettitt test finds the year a change started. We chose this method on evidence: 7 methods were run on 20,000 simulated places with no real trend, and only this one kept false alarms near 5% (3.3 to 7.5%) while still finding real trends. Popular alternatives raised false alarms up to 25% or missed most real trends.

Evidence levels: strong (below 1%), moderate (below 5%), not proven (the rest). The exact number is always shown as “Could this be luck?”: how often a trend this strong would appear if nothing were really changing. “Not proven” is never shown as “no change”.

What can we still grow? (crop model)

One XGBoost model per crop, trained on where 40 crops are grown today (IFPRI MapSPAM 2020, 22,450 farmland cells on every continent), each place described by 10 climate features from all six NASA POWER measurements plus the share of irrigation. On regions held out of training the model tells places that grow a crop from places that do not with AUC 0.90; trained without Egypt, it recognizes Egypt’s real crop list (FAOSTAT) with AUC 0.93. In rain-only mode a rain gate keeps deserts from being rated as rain-fed farmland. The 8,000 trees are packed so the model runs with numpy only (published on Hugging Face).

Projections

Each climate feature follows its own 2001 to 2025 trend to 2030 and 2035, the crop model is scored again on that climate, and the 95% range comes from 200 block resamples of the years. Every future view says “if current trends continue”. It is not a climate forecast.

System

NASA POWER (live and cached) → trend engine and crop outlook (Python) → FastAPI server with documented endpoints → browser app (MapLibre GL JS globe, Plotly charts, plain JavaScript). One server process; hosted on Render. 69 automated tests run on every push.

5. Benefits and intended impact

  • Farmers and agricultural investors: “What can I grow here in 10 years? Where is farmland gaining or losing potential?”
  • Government planners: “Which farming regions are under growing climate stress and need support, which gain potential?”
  • Students, journalists and citizens: “Is my city really getting hotter or drier, or is it just a few unusual years?”

Why it matters here: the Middle East and North Africa is the most water-scarce region in the world (World Bank, 2018); the Mediterranean is already about 1.5 °C warmer than pre-industrial times and warming faster than the global average (MedECC, 2020); over Egypt, NASA’s stations show +0.42 °C per decade since 2001; and 17.5% of Egyptian workers work in agriculture (ILO estimate, 2025). The tool is free, open and honest by design: projections are never shown as forecasts and causes are never claimed.

What makes it different. Most trend tools stop at a map. We add evidence you can trust (false alarms tested and controlled), a check of every trend against an independent NASA record, and the step from “it is changing” to “this is what it means for what you can grow”.

7. Limitations

  • Grid cells are about 55 km: regional screening, not advice for a single farm.
  • NASA POWER warms more slowly than NASA’s stations over Egypt, so many cells read “not proven”; the app shows the station trend next to it.
  • Irrigated mode assumes water can be supplied; it does not show that water exists.
  • The crop model learns where crops are grown today, which also reflects markets, policy and water, not only climate. Olive and date palm are not separate crops in the training maps.
  • Projections continue today’s trends; they are not climate forecasts. No causes are claimed.
  • Free hosting sleeps after 15 minutes without visitors: the first visit can take about a minute.

8. Future plans

  • Finer satellite layers (MODIS and VIIRS at 1 km) to see single fields and towns.
  • Water availability (NASA GRACE groundwater, river flow), so irrigated answers say where water exists.
  • Heat extremes (hot days per year) next to averages, and an Arabic interface.
  • More countries for the farming tabs, and a downloadable report for planners.
